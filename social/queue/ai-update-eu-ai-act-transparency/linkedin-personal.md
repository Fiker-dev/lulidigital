Three days ago a rule quietly went live that touches almost every founder using AI, and most of the headlines about it were the wrong headlines.

On 2 August the EU AI Act's transparency rules — Article 50 — became enforceable. The short version: if people talk to your AI, it has to tell them it's AI. If your marketing uses AI-generated images, voice, or video, that has to be labelled too. It applies whether you're in Berlin or Boston, as long as someone in the EU can reach it. Fines run to €15M or 3% of global turnover.

Everyone's reading that as a headache. I read it as a head start.

Because the disclosure was always the trustworthy move, law or no law. The bot that opens with "you're chatting with our AI assistant" reads as more honest, not less capable. The founder who tags their AI-made ad looks like they have nothing to hide. The rule just made the honest version the default.

Here's how you actually use it, and it's about twenty minutes of work:

- If you run a chatbot, put one line on the first screen: you're talking to AI. Not buried in the footer.
- If you use AI images, audio, or video in your marketing, add a small "AI-generated" tag.
- Assume it applies to you even outside the EU — if an EU customer can load your site, you're in.

The teams that treat this as a trust signal instead of a compliance box will look better than the ones who got caught pretending a bot was a person.

Draw the line before you have to.
