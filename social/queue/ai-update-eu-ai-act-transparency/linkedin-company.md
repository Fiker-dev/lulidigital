The EU AI Act's transparency rules took effect on 2 August. If a business runs an AI that people interact with, that system now has to disclose it's AI. AI-generated images, audio, video, and text have to be labelled as artificial. The reach is extraterritorial — an EU user loading the page is enough — and penalties run to €15M or 3% of global turnover.

The compliance summaries have this backwards. The story isn't the fine. It's that "tell people when it's a machine" just became the baseline for anyone doing business in or into Europe.

That lands cleanly for most small teams, because the honest version was always the better product:

- A chatbot that says "you're speaking with our AI assistant" up front sets the right expectation and hands off to a human faster.
- Marketing that labels its AI-generated assets signals confidence, not weakness.
- One clear disclosure beats a paragraph of legal hedging every time.

Worth noting what didn't change: the rules for high-risk AI systems were pushed to December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus. Transparency wasn't. It's live now.

The work here is small — a line of copy, a label, a quick audit of where AI touches your customer. The teams that do it this month will read as more trustworthy for it, not less.
