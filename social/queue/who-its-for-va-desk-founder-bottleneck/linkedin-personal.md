Somewhere in the last year you became the bottleneck, and nobody told you.

It happens quietly. You're the one who knows which client hates phone calls, where the contract template lives, why that invoice is really two weeks late. So everything routes through you — not because you want control, but because you're the only one holding the context.

Here's the trap. That's exactly the work that feels too fiddly to hand off. Explaining it takes longer than doing it. So you keep doing it, at 11pm, after the real work is done.

The average knowledge worker loses about 11 hours a week just to email (that's McKinsey's number). For a founder holding all the context, it's worse — every reschedule, every follow-up, every "quick question" lands on you.

Two things actually help here:

A person who learns your context — client tone, priorities, the judgement calls — so it stops living only in your head.

And software that takes the repeatable half — drafts, reminders, routing, overnight prep — so that person isn't buried in busywork either.

That pairing is the whole idea behind how we built our VA Desk: human care and AI speed working from the same brief. But you don't need us to start. Start by writing down the five things only you know. That list is your bottleneck — and every item on it can leave your head.
