I watched Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Shopify and Coinbase all agree on the same thing last week, and almost nobody I know clocked it.

The Linux Foundation launched the x402 Foundation — an open standard that lets AI agents actually pay for things. Not draft the invoice. Pay it. Buy the API call, top up the ad spend, check out the reorder, over the same web your browser already uses. Forty companies signed on, most of the names that move money among them.

Here's the part that matters for the rest of us. For two years "AI agent" has meant something that recommends and then waits for you to click. This is the plumbing for the version that clicks itself.

That's not a reason to panic, and it's really not a reason to wire your card to a bot this week. It's a reason to do one small thing now, while it's still theoretical: decide where you'd let software spend money, and where you never would.

Mine's simple. Anything reversible and capped — a stock photo, a metered API — an agent can handle inside a limit I set. Anything with a supplier, a contract, or a number big enough to sting, I sign it myself.

The tools are about to make spending frictionless. The founders who do well won't be the ones who adopt it first. They'll be the ones who drew that line before they had to.

Worth drawing yours this week.
