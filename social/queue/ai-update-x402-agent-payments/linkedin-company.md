Payments just became something AI agents can use on their own.

Last week the Linux Foundation launched the x402 Foundation, an open standard that lets agents and applications send and receive payments over the web the same way they already exchange data. The membership list is the story: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Stripe, Shopify, AWS, Google and Coinbase, among forty organisations. When the companies that run the rails agree on a standard, it stops being a demo.

For most small teams this changes nothing this quarter. What it changes is the question worth asking now.

Until now, an AI agent could research a supplier, draft the reorder, and fill the cart — then stop, because a person had to pay. That pause was doing quiet work. It was the last checkpoint before money moved. As it disappears, it has to be replaced on purpose, not by accident.

The practical move is boring, and it matters: before you let any system spend, define the ceiling and the exceptions. Reversible, capped, low-stakes — fair game inside a limit. Contracts, suppliers, anything that damages a relationship when it's wrong — a person signs.

Automation earns trust by being predictable. Spending is where predictable gets expensive to get wrong.
