The task you keep putting off is almost always the one worth automating first.

When most of us finally decide to automate something, we reach for the exciting part. The lead-gen engine. The content machine. The clever sales sequence we've been meaning to build. It feels like progress because it's the fun problem.

Meanwhile the thing you actually dread — reconciling the numbers, chasing the unpaid invoice, copying this week's figures into the same report for the fourth time — sits there untouched. Too boring to bother with, apparently. So you keep doing it by hand, a little resentfully, every single week.

That's backwards. The dread is the signal. A task you avoid is usually repetitive, rule-based, and low on judgment — the exact profile of work a machine handles well. The projects you're excited about are often the ones that still need your taste.

So this week, don't automate the thing you're excited about. Automate the thing you're avoiding. You get the hours back, and the Sunday-night dread leaves with them.
