Most founders, when they finally decide to automate something, reach for the exciting part first. Lead gen. Content. The sales sequence they've been meaning to build for a year.

I'd start somewhere far less glamorous: the task you dread. The recurring one you keep sliding into Sunday because it's dull, it's fiddly, and only you seem able to do it.

A founder I worked with spent every Friday afternoon assembling the same client update — pulling numbers, reformatting, chasing the one figure that never exported cleanly. Not hard work. Just work that ate the end of every week and quietly followed her home. We didn't touch her marketing. We took that one report and made it assemble itself, so she only had to read it and add the two sentences that actually needed her judgment.

She got her Friday afternoons back. That was the whole win, and it was bigger than it sounds.

Here's the part most people get backwards. The best first automation usually isn't the highest-value task on paper — it's the highest-friction one in your real week. The job that's too small to hire for, too repetitive to enjoy, and too dependent on you to ever go away on its own. Clear that, and you don't just save the hours. You get back the part of the week where the real thinking was supposed to happen.

So don't start with the work you're excited about. Start with the work you keep avoiding.
