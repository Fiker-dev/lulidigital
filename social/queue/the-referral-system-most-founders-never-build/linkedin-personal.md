The last client who told you they were glad they hired you — they'd have handed you a referral on the spot. All it needed was a name. You almost certainly didn't get one.

Not because they didn't mean it. Because the moment passed, nobody made it easy, and everyone got on with their week.

Here's the part that took me too long to see: a thin referral pipeline usually isn't a sign your clients aren't happy enough. It's a missing system. The happiest client in the world still has to remember to refer you, think of the right person, and work out how to introduce you without it feeling awkward — all unprompted, in the middle of their day. That's enough friction to lose almost anyone.

So the fix isn't charm or nerve. It's doing the remembering for them:

- Ask at the peak — right after they've felt the value, not three months later.
- Hand them the words — a two-line intro they can forward as-is, so there's no blank page to fill.
- Close the loop — tell them how it went. People who feel seen refer again.

An afternoon to build once. After that, the warmest leads you'll ever get stop depending on whether a happy client happened to think of you at the right moment.

And they're better clients, too. A Journal of Marketing study that tracked around 10,000 customers found referred ones were about 16% more valuable and noticeably less likely to leave. Your best channel is already sitting in your inbox. Most of us just never build the thing that turns it on.
