The Brushfire Problem: How Small Operational Failures Burn Down Your Week

"The goal is not to prevent every fire. It is to build fire doors."

One missed handoff becomes a delayed deliverable. That becomes a client chase. The client chase pulls you out of a planning session. By Thursday the week you planned on Sunday is gone. This is not a productivity problem — it is a systems problem. And the visible crisis is almost never the root cause. It is the symptom of a gap upstream: a process that was never documented, an approval living in someone's inbox, a task held together by one person's memory.

Before reaching for any tool, run a fast audit. Look at your handoffs — what happens when the person responsible is unavailable? Look at your approvals — which decisions genuinely need you, and which ones land in your lap only because no rule exists? Look at your recurring tasks — if the person who does them left tomorrow, would anyone know how? The answers tell you exactly where the brushfire risk is highest. Tools layered on a broken process just make the process faster and more broken. Fix the process first, then automate on top of something clean.

The full piece covers how to spot the compounding pattern, a checklist of operational signals that the spread is already underway, what to automate versus systemise versus keep human, and where AI agents fit into a stable operation.
