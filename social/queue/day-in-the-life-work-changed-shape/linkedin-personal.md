A year ago my Thursday was a to-do list I could see the bottom of. Answer the emails. Chase the invoice. Reformat the deck. Move the numbers from one sheet into the other. Tiring, but honest — you always knew when you were done.

Now most of that runs on its own. The inbox is sorted and drafted before I open the laptop. The invoice chases itself. The report builds overnight.

Everyone assumes that hands you back a quiet, empty afternoon. It doesn't. It hands you the harder half of the job.

Because the busywork was also where I hid. Sorting the inbox felt like progress. Now it's sorted by 8am, and the thing left on my desk is the question I used to postpone: is this the right client, is this the right price, is this thing we're building actually any good.

The machine took the part that had a bottom. What's left is the part that doesn't — deciding, judging, saying no, holding the standard when nobody's checking.

I won't pretend I miss the busywork. I don't. But nobody warns you that automating the easy work doesn't make the day lighter. It makes it sharper. Less time doing, more time being responsible for what got done.

That's the trade. I'd take it every time. Just don't expect the free afternoon — expect the better questions.
