The founders getting the most out of AI this year aren't the ones who automated the most. They're the ones who automated the right layer.

Most of your day is glue. Copying a number from one tab into another. Chasing the person who owes you a reply. Reformatting notes into an update nobody reads carefully. Routing a message to whoever handles that. None of it needs you — it just needs doing, and it quietly eats the hours when you'd actually think.

That's the layer to hand over. The status updates, the tagging, the chasing, the first draft of everything. Give the machine the glue.

What you keep is the judgment. The call on money. The wording of the hard email to a client. The decision that has a reputation attached to it. The moment where 90% right isn't good enough. Those don't get faster when you automate them — they get riskier.

Here's the part people miss: automating the glue doesn't just save time. It hands you back the hours you were too buried to spend on the decisions only you can make. That's the whole point. Not doing more — doing the work that was always yours, with room to do it well.

So before you automate anything, sort the task into one of two piles. Glue, or judgment. Automate the first pile without guilt. Guard the second one like it's the job — because it is.
