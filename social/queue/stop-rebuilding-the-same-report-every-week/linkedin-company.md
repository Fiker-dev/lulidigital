New on the blog.
You Spend Every Monday Rebuilding the Same Report. The Numbers Were Already There.

"The problem was never that you needed the report. It's that you became the machine that builds it — every week, by hand, from the same sources that could have delivered it themselves."

Manual reporting is sneaky because it never feels optional and always feels productive — a tidy artifact at the end, a box ticked. But most of those hours aren't spent thinking. They're spent fetching, pasting and reconciling numbers that already exist. The fix isn't a six-month data project: connect the sources once, define the report as a template instead of a task, and put the human on judgment, not assembly. The ritual shrinks from ninety minutes of building to ten minutes of reading — and gets more frequent and more trustworthy at the same time.
