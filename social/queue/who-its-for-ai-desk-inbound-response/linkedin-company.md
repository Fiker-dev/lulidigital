Most businesses don't lose leads at the pitch. They lose them in the gap between the enquiry landing and anyone reading it.

The research here is unusually blunt. In the MIT lead-response study, contacting a new lead within five minutes made it 21 times more likely to qualify than waiting thirty. Same lead, same offer — the only variable was speed. And 78% of buyers go with whoever answers first.

For a small team, five-minute replies sound impossible. Someone has to be watching the inbox, awake, and free, all at once. So the realistic version becomes "we'll get to it" — and "we'll get to it" is where warm leads quietly go cold.

This is what the AI Desk handles. Every inbound enquiry is read the moment it arrives, sorted by what it actually needs, and answered with a drafted reply and a booking link — routed to a person for approval, never fired blind. The founder still decides. They just stop being the bottleneck between a warm lead and a fast, human answer.

Speed to first reply is one of the cheapest advantages a small company has. Most never set it up.
