Every client I've lost, I lost in the first two weeks. I just didn't find out until months later.

Here's the uncomfortable part: it was never the work. The work was good. What went cold was the start — the welcome email written from scratch at 9pm, the logins I asked for three separate times, the kickoff call booked whenever someone remembered to send the link.

None of it was bad. It just felt held together by hand. And a new client can't judge your delivery yet — the work hasn't started — so they judge the only thing they can see: how calm and organised those first days feel.

What finally fixed it wasn't charm or longer hours. It was building the first week once:

- One warm welcome that says what happens next and bundles everything I need into a single ask, not five scattered ones
- One visible win inside the first seven days, decided in advance, so value lands before doubt does
- One owner for the checklist, so it runs the same whether I'm rested or slammed

Took an afternoon to set up. Every client since has started on a calmer footing — and I stopped losing good people to a bad start I never meant to give them.

If you can feel your onboarding being held together by hand, that's not a flaw in you. It's a system you haven't written down yet.
