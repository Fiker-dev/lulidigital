Most client relationships don't end at the renewal. They end in week one — quietly, months before anyone names it.

The reason is simple. When a deal closes, the client is paying closer attention than they ever will again, and they have no delivery to judge you on yet. So they judge the only thing visible: how organised and effortless those first days feel. A shaky onboarding rarely produces a complaint. It produces a client who's a little slower to respond, a little quicker to "circle back internally," and eventually gone — without ever leaving the feedback that would have let you fix it.

That silence is expensive, because keeping a client costs a fraction of winning one. Early churn is where retention is actually lost, long before any renewal conversation.

The mistake most teams make: they treat sales as a designed process and onboarding as improvisation. Reverse it. A repeatable first two weeks does three jobs — it removes uncertainty, it delivers one visible win fast, and it makes every ask effortless on the client's side. Calm competence, delivered on schedule, outperforms charm delivered at random.

Build the first week once. Give it an owner. Then every client gets the same calm start, instead of whatever version you had energy for the day they signed.
