A broken automation never calls to tell you it stopped. That's the entire problem.

When a person drops a task, someone chases it — a client, a colleague, a gap in the week you can feel. When an automation drops a task, nothing happens. No error, no complaint. The step just quietly stops while everything downstream carries on as if it ran. You're the last to find out, sometimes weeks later.

The fix isn't cleverer automation. It's giving each one a heartbeat — a small signal that proves it's still doing its job:

- Name the proof. For every workflow, decide what "it worked" actually looks like: an invoice in the sent folder, a new row in the sheet, a lead in the sequence.
- Make it report. An automation that tells you "12 leads added this week" is one you'll notice the week it says zero.
- Book ten minutes. Once a week, someone glances at the heartbeats — not to do the work, just to confirm it's still happening.

That's the whole difference between automation you hope is working and automation you know is. Set it up right and the quiet means it's running — not that no one's listening.
