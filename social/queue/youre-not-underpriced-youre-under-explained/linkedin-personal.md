Most deals you think you lost on price, you lost on clarity.

The buyer couldn't tell what they were actually getting. So they did the only thing left — compared numbers. And a number always loses to a smaller number.

For a long time I read "you're a bit expensive" as a pricing problem. Drop the rate, win the work. It never worked. The ones who pushed hardest on price were the ones who couldn't picture the outcome. They weren't cheap. They were unsure.

The fix was never a discount. It was making the result impossible to misread:

- What they'll have at the end, in plain words — not "a strategy," but "a booking system that texts the client back in under a minute."
- What changes on their side once it's running.
- What it keeps costing them to do it the current way.

Do that and price stops being the conversation. It turns into a line item on a decision they've already made.

You're probably not underpriced. You're under-explained.
