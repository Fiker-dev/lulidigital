**Post 1/2**
Automation doesn't remove accountability. It relocates it.

When Air Canada's chatbot invented a refund policy, the airline argued the bot was its own legal entity. The tribunal disagreed — you own everything on your site. The machine ran the task; the business owned the outcome.

**Post 2/2**
So the delegate-vs-automate line isn't "human or machine." It's: when this fails silently, who notices, and who's accountable?

Clear input, no relationship at stake → automate it. Judgment or your name on the output → keep a person in the seat.
