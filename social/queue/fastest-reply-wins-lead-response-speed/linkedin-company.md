The most expensive number in most funnels never shows up on a spreadsheet: the minutes between a lead arriving and someone answering it.

Here's the economics nobody runs. You pay — in ads, content, referrals, SEO — to earn each enquiry. When one goes cold because it sat in an inbox during a meeting, that cost doesn't refund itself. You bought the interest and let it evaporate on the doorstep. Do that across a month and you're funding a slow leak that never appears as a line item, because a lead lost to silence doesn't complain. It just books someone else.

The research here is unusually blunt. Harvard Business Review's analysis of 1.25 million leads found firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times more likely to reach a decision-maker — and more than sixty times more likely than the ones who waited a day. An MIT study put the odds of even making contact at roughly 100 times higher at five minutes than at thirty.

The reflex is to read a slow pipeline as a volume problem and go buy more traffic. Usually it's a response problem in disguise, and it's cheaper to fix the leak than to keep buying water.

The fix isn't heroics. It's removing the wait:

- Acknowledge instantly and automatically, so the first touch never depends on who happens to be free.
- Qualify in the moment, with the two questions that sort a serious buyer from a browser.
- Hand off to a human for the part that needs judgment — the machine buys the time, the person wins the relationship.

Speed isn't about working faster. It's about making sure the most valuable minutes in your funnel don't hang on a busy person's spare attention.
