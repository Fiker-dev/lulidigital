Someone filled in my contact form while I was on a call. I saw it two hours later, wrote back something thoughtful — and never heard from them again. At the time I filed it under "not a serious lead." I was wrong. They were serious for about five minutes, and I'd slept through the window.

That's the part nobody warns you about with new enquiries: they have a shelf life, and it's shorter than a lunch break. Interest peaks the second someone reaches out, then cools fast while they open three other tabs and start comparing you to everyone else.

There's a Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million leads that puts hard numbers on it. Reply within an hour and you're nearly seven times more likely to actually reach a decision-maker than if you wait even sixty minutes longer. A separate MIT study found five minutes beats thirty by a wide margin. The reply that wins usually isn't the cleverest one. It's the first one.

Here's the part that stung. I'd been telling myself I had a lead problem, and spending more to get more enquiries. I didn't. I had a response problem wearing a lead problem's clothes, and the leads I already paid for were quietly cooling in my inbox.

So here's how you can actually use this, this week:

- Time your real response for five days — when the enquiry lands versus when someone replies. The gap is uglier than you'd guess.
- Write one warm, instant acknowledgement and wire it to send the moment a form comes in, so it goes out at 2pm and 2am alike.
- Give the human follow-up one owner and a one-hour clock. If it's everyone's job, it's no one's.

You already paid to earn the enquiry. The whole game is answering it while it's still warm.
