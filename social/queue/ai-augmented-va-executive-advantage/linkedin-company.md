Your VA Isn't the Problem. The Way You're Using Them Is.

"The jump from task-taker to executive partner happens at Layer 2. Most people never make it past Layer 1."

Most VA relationships run like a ticket queue. You raise a request. It gets handled. You raise another. The ceiling on that model isn't your VA's ability — it's the fact that nothing moves until you do. Every follow-up, every brief, every delegation still starts with you.

The shift worth making is from task execution to workflow ownership. That means the assistant owns a whole process — client onboarding, reporting cycles, follow-up sequences — not just individual to-dos inside it. And at the layer above that: anticipation. Meeting prep ready before you open the invite. A flag on the client who hasn't replied in ten days. A draft waiting for your approval, not your composition. None of this is futuristic. It's just not how most executives have set things up yet.

The full piece walks through the three support layers, a quick audit to run on your own working week, what to automate versus what always needs a human eye, and the setup steps that make the whole thing compound over time.
