The second time you type the same answer, stop typing.

That repeated explanation — how you handle rush requests, why the invoice is structured that way, what "done" actually means on a project — is the most valuable documentation your business will ever have. And it writes itself, if you let it.

The rule is small enough to actually keep: the first time a client or teammate asks something, you answer. The second time, you answer *and* paste it into one shared doc. The third time, you send the link.

Nobody sits down to "build a knowledge base." It feels like a project for a quieter week that never comes. But answer-twice-then-link costs nothing extra and compounds fast. In a month you've captured the twenty questions that eat your week, in your own words — ready for a new teammate, or an AI assistant, to handle next time without you in the loop.

The knowledge was always there. You were just retyping it.
