The safest operating theatres in the world don't run on better equipment. They run on a checklist nobody is allowed to skip.

Before anaesthesia, the team confirms the same things every time — identity, allergies, airway risk, whether the suction and emergency drugs are ready. Not because they forget. Because "we're sure" is the exact moment things get missed. The WHO built that checklist after realising the failures weren't about skill. They were about skipped steps.

Software learned the same lesson and gave it new names: production readiness reviews, evals, pre-mortems, the NIST AI risk framework. Different theatre, identical instinct — verify the failure modes before you expose anyone to them.

Most teams reach for another tool when what they actually need is a step they refuse to skip. A system you can trust isn't the one with the most features. It's the one that was checked before it shipped, every time — including the times everyone was certain it was fine.

That discipline is what we build into every system we hand a client: the rollback, the alert, the failure they'll never see because it was planned for. The founder who trusts the work isn't trusting the tool. They're trusting the check behind it.
