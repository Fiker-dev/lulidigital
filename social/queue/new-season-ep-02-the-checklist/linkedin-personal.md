Before I ever put anyone under, I ran the same checklist. Every single time.

Nurse anaesthetist training drills one thing into you harder than anything else: you check before you commit. Identity. Allergies. Airway. Is the suction working. Are the emergency drugs drawn and labelled. You do it even when you're certain — *especially* when you're certain, because certainty is exactly when people skip a step and someone gets hurt.

I don't put people to sleep anymore. I build systems. But that habit came with me, and it turned out to be the most valuable thing I own.

Here's how it actually shows up: nothing I build for a client goes live until it's passed a pre-flight check. What happens when this breaks at 2am? Where's the rollback? Who gets told? What's the failure the client will never see because I already planned for it? That's not caution slowing the work down. That's the reason people trust it.

The tech world already knows this — it just uses different words. Production readiness reviews. Evals. Pre-mortems. All of it is the same instinct a theatre team has: don't trust the thing until you've checked the thing.

So if you're shipping anything to clients, here's the part you can actually use today: you don't need another tool. You need a checklist you refuse to skip. Write down the five things that would embarrass you if they broke. Check them every time — even when you're sure.

Same standards. Different theatre.
