Most automations don't fail because the tool is weak. They fail because nobody wrote down what "done right" looks like before switching it on.

So here's the ten-minute habit that fixes it: before you automate a task, write the finish line. Not the steps — the standard. What does a good result look like? What should never happen? What's the one case where it has to stop and ask you?

"Sort my inbox" hands you a pile you still have to re-check. "Move anything from a client into Priority, flag anything that mentions money or a deadline, leave the rest" hands you something you can trust by 8am.

The finish line is the whole job. Write it once, in plain words, and give the machine that — not a vague topic. The automation gets sharper, and you stop being the person who quietly redoes its work every morning.

Ten minutes, before you build. It's the cheapest quality control you'll ever run.
