Most automations don't fail because the tool is weak. They fail because nobody wrote down what "done right" looks like before switching it on.

Here's the pattern I see over and over. A founder maps a process in their head — onboarding, invoicing, lead follow-up — and hands it to a tool to run. The tool does exactly what it's told. The trouble is nobody ever told it the part that lived in your head: the exception you always catch, the "wait, not for this client," the quiet judgment call you make without noticing you're making it.

When a person ran that process, they filled those gaps silently. Automate it and the gaps don't disappear — they just stop being handled. The dashboard still shows green while the work quietly goes wrong.

So before you automate anything, write the process down by hand first. Not the happy path — the whole thing:
- The trigger, and every step after it
- The decision points, and what you'd do at each one
- The single place a mistake would actually hurt — keep a human there
- What "finished correctly" looks like, in one sentence

If you can't write that last sentence, the tool can't either. That's not an automation problem. It's a clarity problem, and no software fixes it for you.

The best automations aren't the cleverest ones. They're the ones built on a process somebody already understood cold.
