Most clients don't leave because the work was bad. They leave because, three weeks in, they quietly stopped feeling sure they made the right call.

That gap — between the day someone signs and the day they actually feel confident — is where you keep them or lose them. And for most of us it's still held together by memory. A welcome email here, a kickoff call someone promised, an intake form we'll chase "once things calm down."

Nothing dramatic breaks. It just adds up to a first month that feels less polished than the pitch that won them.

Here's the part worth stealing: you don't need a new hire or a new platform to close it. You need the predictable things to happen on their own. Contract signed kicks off the welcome sequence. The intake form sends itself and nudges at 48 hours. The Day 7 and Day 30 check-ins get queued so nobody falls through.

Then you keep your real attention for the moments that need a person — the first reply to a worried client, the scope change, the win worth celebrating properly.

Automate the part that's the same every time. Stay human for the part that isn't. That's the whole system.

Pick your most manual onboarding step and build one trigger around it this week. Small fixes compound faster than you'd think.
