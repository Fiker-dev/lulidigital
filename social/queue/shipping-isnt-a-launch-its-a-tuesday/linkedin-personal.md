Everyone waiting for their big launch is quietly avoiding the small one they could ship today.

For years I thought building meant working toward a moment — the reveal, the announcement, the day it all goes live and the room finally claps. So I saved things up. Polished in private. Waited until it felt worthy of a launch.

The launches, when they came, were fine. Nice, even. But they never moved the business the way I expected. What moved it was the boring part I wasn't counting: the tiny thing shipped on an ordinary Tuesday. A cleaner onboarding email. One page rewritten. A workflow that gave a client back twenty minutes. Nobody clapped. Half the time nobody noticed.

That's the part nobody warns you about. A launch is a moment. A business is a habit. The work that compounds doesn't come from the best launch day — it comes from shipping something small enough to actually finish, often enough that it stacks.

My day is built around that now. Not "what's the big thing," but "what's one thing I can put out before this afternoon that's a little better than yesterday." Most days it's unglamorous. Most days that's the point.

Save the launch for when there's genuinely something worth gathering a crowd for. Until then, the work is a Tuesday.

Ship the Tuesday.
