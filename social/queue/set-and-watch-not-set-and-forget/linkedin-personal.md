The scariest automations aren't the ones that crash. They're the ones that keep running after they've stopped working.

A founder I know had a lead form quietly stop adding people to her follow-up sequence. Every enquiry still hit her inbox, so nothing felt broken. Six weeks later she did the maths on what she'd missed — not one lead, a whole season of them. The automation had been doing its job perfectly, into a void.

That's the part nobody warns you about. When a person drops a task, someone chases it. When an automation drops a task, it fails in silence — no error, no complaint, just a step that stops happening while everything downstream carries on as if it didn't.

"Set and forget" was always the wrong promise. The version that holds up is set and watch. Not hovering — building the thing so it tells on itself. A workflow that can quietly stop is a liability. One that pings you the moment it produces nothing is something you can actually trust.

So if your systems run without anyone watching them, that isn't a hands-off business yet. It's a bet that nothing has changed since the day you built it. Something always has.
