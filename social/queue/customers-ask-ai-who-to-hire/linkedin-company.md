The most important sentence about your business is now one you'll never see written. It's the answer an AI assistant gives when a buyer asks it who to hire — and it comes back as one or two names, not a page of ten.

That's the shift worth sitting with. Search gave you a seat in a lineup and let the buyer choose. AI makes the shortlist first. Roughly half of B2B buyers now open a chatbot before they open Google, and the list they act on has quietly shrunk to two or three names. You're on it or you're invisible, with no way to argue your way back in after the fact.

The reflex is to treat this as a new algorithm to game. It isn't. These systems lean on consensus — they cross-check what you say against what everyone else says about you. The Princeton research on generative visibility found the largest gains came from corroborated substance: cited sources, real numbers, third-party proof. Not louder marketing.

Which means the work is the oldest work there is:

- Say plainly who you help and what problem you remove.
- Publish content that answers the questions real buyers actually ask.
- Get vouched for somewhere other than your own site.
- Keep the name, the description, and the details consistent everywhere.

Do that and you're not chasing the machine. You're handing it a clear, credible, consistent answer to the only question that now matters: who does this well?
