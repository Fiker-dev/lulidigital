Last week I typed my own service into ChatGPT and asked it the exact question a new client would — "who should I hire to do this?" — just to see if my business would come up.

It didn't. And here's the part that stayed with me: there was no page two to lose on. The assistant didn't hand back ten links to skim. It named one or two, gave a sentence on why, and that was the entire shortlist. You're in the answer or you're invisible — and you never even find out the conversation happened.

Roughly half of B2B buyers now start there instead of on Google. So this isn't a someday problem.

The fix surprised me most. My instinct was to go polish the website — sharper homepage, better copy. Wrong instinct. These systems run on consensus; they won't just take your word for it. The Princeton research on this found the biggest jumps in getting named came from cited, corroborated substance — real sources, plain facts, third-party proof — not marketing gloss.

So here's how you can actually use this, today:

- Open ChatGPT or Perplexity and ask it who to hire for what you do. Read what it says about you — or sit with the silence.
- Then stop editing your homepage and go get corroborated somewhere else: an honest review, an accurate listing, a mention by someone you've worked with.

The businesses AI recommends aren't the loudest. They're the clearest and the most vouched-for. That part hasn't changed at all.
