You Don't Need More Leads. You Need to Go Back to the Ones Who Already Said Maybe.

"A maybe that went quiet is a very different prospect from a stranger who's never heard of you."

When growth slows, the instinct is to spend more at the top of the funnel. New ads. A new channel. More volume. But most businesses are sitting on a list that's already done the hard part — people who raised their hand, asked a question, booked a call, and then went quiet. They already know the name. They already pictured working with you. Silence isn't a no. It's usually just timing.

The practical move: sort dormant contacts by how far they got, not how recent they are. Someone who saw a proposal is warmer than someone who filled in a form. Lead your message with their original problem, not a "just checking in." Give it a short sequence across a week or two — enough to be noticed, not enough to be annoying. Make the next step tiny: a reply, a yes-or-no question, a five-minute call. The goal is to restart the conversation, not close it in one go.

The full piece covers why dormant leads convert more cheaply than cold ones, the real reason this work never gets done, and how to build a reactivation system that runs without depending on you having a good day.
